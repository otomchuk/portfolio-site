What I think about modash.io

I tried not to judge the site on taste. I looked for real data and real user mentions, and mapped them to what's on the site today.

In short
  1. Half of visitors leave after one page; the other half read about 5. That second group is either evaluating carefully or hunting for something.
  2. Users praise the same few things (vetting, fake-follower checks, all-in-one). The best results that prove it, though, sit on feature pages instead of where people decide.
  3. Three spots could do more selling: product visuals on mobile, the empty half of the sign-up page, and reviews from smaller brands.
01 — TRAFFIC

Two very different kinds of visit

Similarweb shows a 49% bounce rate and 3.05 pages per visit. If 49% of sessions see a single page, the remaining 51% must average about 5 pages each.

49%1 page, then gone
51%~5 pages each
0.49 × 1 + 0.51 × 5.0 ≈ 3.05 pages / visit

Five pages can mean real evaluation: features, pricing, integrations, case studies. It can also mean people are looking for something they can't find, such as pricing details, docs or the login.

…but those 5 pages go by quickly

The average visit lasts 1:44. If one-page visits count as close to zero, the deeper half spends roughly 3.5 minutes across 5 pages, so about 40 seconds per page. That looks more like skimming than reading, which leans towards "looking for something".

03 — OUTCOMES

The payoff is hidden on feature pages

The strongest answers to "is it worth it?" already exist, but you only see them if you click into a specific feature:

380M+creators, so you never run out of profiles
10 minsetup and 100% content coverage, instead of hours spent proving ROI
1–3 daysfor creators to get paid, with automatic invoices and no creator fees

Bringing these to the home page, next to the matching feature, would turn a list of features into a list of outcomes.

04 — VISUALS

On mobile, the product is hard to see

The big, bold titles work well on a phone. The product visuals are small, slightly blurry and sit inside large decorative backgrounds, so they read as decoration rather than "this is what you'll use". Feature pages on mobile also have no product tour.

05 — PROOF

Half of the sign-up page is empty

On desktop, the sign-up page gives half the screen to a gradient. This is the moment someone is deciding to commit, so it's a good place for one proof point.

A quick sketch, not a design. The quote is already on the home page.

06 — TARGETING

Big logos first, peers much later

Victoria's Secret, Birkenstock and NordVPN appear right after the hero. That speaks to enterprise buyers. But many visitors are smaller brands, and the Reddit threads show their worry is price. Reviews from teams their size are more convincing for them, and those sit near the bottom of the page.

Idea worth testing: show a peer story next to the logo wall.
07 — HOW I'D VALIDATE THIS

Everything above is a hypothesis

I'd check it before changing anything:

Who
Influencer marketing managers who don't use Modash yet. A mix of small in-house teams and larger brands.
What
An unmoderated test: modash.io vs hypeauditor.com, in alternating order. "What catches your eye? Would you sign up? What's missing?"
Listen for
Their own words for the job they hire a tool for, and where they hesitate. Good input for headlines.
Check in data
Where the 5-page visitors go, what they search for, and how sign-up starts vs completions compare on mobile and desktop.

I'd also love to learn the company's goals and marketing strategy, to see how close these assumptions are.
